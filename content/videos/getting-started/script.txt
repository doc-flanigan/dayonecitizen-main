Welcome to Day One Citizen, your plain-English guide to Star Citizen. It's written by a veteran backer, for someone who has never even seen the game.

This is Step One. You have an account, and you've downloaded the launcher. Maybe you bought the game. Maybe you're trying a free event. Either way, you're now staring at a screen wondering what comes next. I've been there. Let's walk through it together.

The RSI Launcher is Roberts Space Industries' own application. Think of it like Steam or Epic, but for Star Citizen only. Log in with the email and password from your RSI account.

Before you click anything, look at the version number. Star Citizen is still in alpha, which means the game is being built while people play it. Right now it starts with four point ten. Patches arrive every few weeks, and that's normal.

Near the big Launch button you'll see a channel selector. Live is the stable public version, and that's where almost every player is. The test servers are for trying upcoming patches. They're buggier, they have fewer players, and they install as a separate copy of the game. Stick to Live for now.

Click Launch. The first load can take several minutes. That's normal too. Grab a drink.

Once the game loads, you'll create your character. There are only two choices. First is your pilot name, which defaults to your RSI handle. Treat it as permanent, because changing it later isn't instant. Second is your appearance. Almost none of it affects gameplay, and you can change it later at medical facilities and barber shops. Pick something and move on.

Now for your starting city. You don't actually choose it here. It's set by your game package, or assigned to you automatically. You'll land in one of four big cities in the Stanton system. Lorville is on Hurston. Area Eighteen is on ArcCorp. New Babbage is on microTech. Orison is on Crusader. All four work fine on day one, and each one has a route to a spaceport where your ship waits.

Before you do anything else, let's look at settings. Press Escape to open the menu, then open Options. If the game feels choppy, lower your graphics quality preset one step. Star Citizen is demanding, and busy cities are the hardest places for any computer. Field of view is personal comfort, so adjust it until it feels right.

Next, Key Bindings. Don't change much yet. If you do experiment, export your current bindings first, so you can restore them. For now you need only a few keys. F is interact. F1 opens your mobiGlas, the wrist computer I'll explain in a moment. I opens your inventory. Our keybinds guide on dayonecitizen dot com has the full list.

You start in your hab. That's a small apartment in your city, like a hotel room in space. There's a bed, a storage terminal for your gear, and sometimes a news screen on the wall. The bed matters, because it's how you log out safely. We'll come back to that.

Press F1 to open your mobiGlas. It's your phone, map, wallet, and mission board in one. Open the wallet. New characters start with twenty thousand A U E C. That's the in-game money you earn and spend, and it can be reset during alpha. If you signed up with a referral code, you'll also have fifty thousand UEC. That's a different, permanent account currency. Don't mix the two up.

Open the Contracts app and look around, but don't accept anything yet. Then close the mobiGlas. Walk to the door and press F to open it. Take the elevator down to the city. From there, head to the spaceport and find an A S O P terminal. That's a tall kiosk with a ship icon, usually near the hangars. Hold F, choose your ship, and claim it. The terminal gives you a hangar number. Our getting around guide shows the route for every city.

A few things you'll want to know that nobody tells you.

Tap F to use things. Hold F on an object or terminal for a longer menu of options. In a ship, E rolls you to the right. Doors and terminals use F.

Dying isn't total loss. When you respawn, you get back what you had equipped, meaning your armor and weapons. But anything in your backpack and pockets stays on your body where you died. So don't carry everything you own until you know what you're doing.

When you finish playing, don't just quit. Find a bed, hold F on it, and choose Bed Log. That saves your location. If you log out from the Escape menu instead, you'll start next time at your home spawn point.

And things will break. You might fall through a floor, or a mission might bug out. This is alpha. If the game crashes or acts strange after an update, open the launcher and use Verify. It checks your files and repairs anything broken. It can take a while, often fifteen to thirty minutes, and it fixes a surprising number of problems. If you get lost in a city, press Escape and use Respawn to return to your hab. And if a ship bug strands you, filing an insurance claim can sometimes work around it. Filing a claim can cost some A U E C, depending on the ship, so keep that in mind.

That's your first session. You can launch the game, set it up, find your way around your hab, and claim your ship. That's more than most people figure out on their own.
